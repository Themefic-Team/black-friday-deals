## How to Use This Collection

Start with a project, not a discount. A dashboard template might be useful if you are building an admin panel this quarter; an annual subscription may be poor value if you only need it for a week.

1. **Choose a category that matches your task.** The listings cover building, designing, hosting, marketing, and learning.
2. **Check the listing’s status.** Every current row is a preview. “Not announced” is not an active offer.
3. **Read the vendor’s terms.** When offers become available, check eligibility, included features, renewal pricing, and the end date at the source.
4. **Compare the total commitment.** Include taxes, extra seats, usage charges, and migration effort in your decision.

This guide is intended for independent developers, design teams, agency owners, and people maintaining their own products. You do not need to buy a new tool to benefit from the season: a free tier or an existing license may already cover the job.

## How We Evaluate Deals

The following checklist defines the review standard for future submissions. **It is not a claim that the showcase products have been tested, purchased, or verified.** Confirming an advertised promotion is also different from reviewing the quality of the product itself.

### 1. Find an Official Source

An offer should be supported by a vendor-controlled page, public announcement, or checkout terms. A coupon copied from another directory is a lead to investigate, not enough evidence to label a deal confirmed.

### 2. Check the Price Comparison

Record which plan is discounted and what price the promotion is compared against. Distinguish a temporary sale from ordinary annual-billing savings. If the regular price cannot be established, describe the advertised price without asserting a verified saving.

### 3. Read Eligibility and Exclusions

Check whether the promotion applies to new customers, existing subscribers, upgrades, or specific billing cycles. Note excluded plans, minimum purchases, seat requirements, and whether a coupon can be combined with other offers.

### 4. Explain the Commitment

Separate one-time purchases from subscriptions. For recurring plans, record whether the discount lasts for one payment, a fixed period, or subsequent renewals. For “lifetime” offers, check what the vendor says about updates, support, usage limits, and the lifetime being promised.

### 5. Establish the Sale Window

Use the vendor’s stated start and end dates, including the time zone when provided. Do not carry last year’s coupon or deadline into a new listing. If an end date is unspecified, say so instead of inventing one.

### 6. Keep the Status Honest

Use these labels as the collection moves beyond its showcase stage:

- **Preview:** A product example with no confirmed promotion in this collection.
- **Scheduled:** An official promotion has been announced but has not started.
- **Active:** The published offer has started and its terms have been checked against an official source.
- **Expired:** The published end date has passed or the vendor has withdrawn the offer.
- **Needs review:** A reported discrepancy means the listing should not be relied on until checked again.

A confirmed listing should include its source and the date of the latest check. If the displayed price or checkout behavior disagrees with the listing, flag it for review rather than presenting it as a working deal.

## Before You Buy

A large discount can still be the wrong purchase. Use this short checklist before checking out:

- **Name the task.** What will you use the product for in the next few months?
- **Try the relevant feature.** A polished demo does not establish that your integration or export workflow will work.
- **Calculate the full cost.** Include seats, add-ons, usage charges, taxes, and the price after the promotional period.
- **Check ownership and access.** Can you export your data or keep using downloaded assets if you stop paying?
- **Read the license.** Confirm whether your intended commercial work, client delivery, or redistribution is permitted.
- **Review cancellation and refunds.** A discounted purchase may have different refund terms from a standard subscription.
- **Avoid duplicate spending.** Compare the purchase with tools and licenses you already have.

For example, a $10-per-month service billed annually costs $120 upfront. A 30% discount on that annual bill brings the first payment to $84, but the next renewal may return to $120. Compare both amounts with how often you expect to use the service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are any of these 2026 deals available now?

No offer is confirmed in the current README. The rows are product previews and clearly marked layout placeholders. Check the official vendor website for current pricing; a link here does not establish a discount.

### When should I check for announcements?

Black Friday is November 27 and Cyber Monday is November 30 in 2026. Vendors set their own schedules, so an offer may begin earlier or finish later. Use the announced dates for each promotion rather than assuming every sale runs over the same weekend.

### Can I use a coupon from a previous year?

Do not assume an old code still works or will return. A code should only appear as a current promotion after its eligibility and validity have been checked against an official source.

### Does “verified” mean the product has been independently tested?

No. Offer verification concerns the advertised price, eligibility, and timing. Hands-on testing, security assessments, and product comparisons are separate activities and should only be claimed when supporting work has actually been done.

### Is the highest percentage discount always the best value?

No. A smaller saving on the plan you need can be more useful than a steep discount on a larger bundle. Compare the actual amount you will pay, the renewal price, and the features you will use.

### Can existing customers use the same offer as new customers?

That depends on the vendor. Look for restrictions on renewals, upgrades, account age, and billing cycles. If the terms do not answer your situation, ask the vendor before paying.

### What if the price changes after a listing is published?

The vendor controls checkout pricing and can change or withdraw an offer. Confirm the final amount before purchase and report discrepancies so the listing can be corrected or marked for review.
